W1700K SDR Getting started

A Wi-Fi 7 router rebuilt as a wideband receiver. Its radio captures raw 14-bit IQ from the 2.4, 5 and 6 GHz bands and streams every sample to your Linux host over Ethernet, where a browser shows the spectrum live.

Sample rate
40 to 320 Msps
Samples
14-bit complex IQ
Bands
2.4 · 5 · 6 GHz
Stream
10 GbE, UDP
Direction
Receive only

What you need

Your unit arrives with the SDR system installed. There is nothing to flash and no serial console to connect: you cable it, set up the host, give the unit your key with a paperclip, and start a stream.

  • The unit and its power adapter.
  • A Linux host with two free Ethernet ports. One carries control (any speed). The other carries the stream: a 10GBASE-T port for everything the receiver has, or a 5 Gbit/s USB adapter built on Realtek's RTL8159 (about 80 dollars) for the modes listed under What your link carries.
  • Two Ethernet cables, the stream's rated for 10 Gbit/s.
  • An SSH key pair on the host. The unit arrives with no login key of anyone's. If you have none, ssh-keygen -t ed25519 makes one.
  • A paperclip, for the reset button. Only a hand on the unit can give it a login key.
  • The card from the box. It carries your unit's serial number and the fingerprint of its SSH host key.

On the host: git, make, a C and C++ compiler, Python 3.10 or newer, OpenSSH, clang with ld.lld, llvm-objcopy and llvm-readobj, openssl, and the FFTW3 and zlib development packages (Debian and Ubuntu: libfftw3-dev, zlib1g-dev).

Five steps to a live spectrum

  1. Cable it and power it on

    Connect the unit's lan3 port to the host's control port and its 10 GbE wan port to the host's stream port. Then plug in the power.

    The unit needs no screen. Its front LED tells you where it is:

    1. White, then steady redThe loader and the kernel are starting.
    2. Rainbow, once roundBooted. Three seconds, out of the dark.
    3. Yellow, breathingThe stream port is agreeing a speed: about 6 s, about 18 s on the USB adapter.
    4. White, brighteningLinked and waiting for you. It fills over six seconds, then holds.

    Yellow that keeps breathing means the stream port has no link: check that cable and the far end.

  2. Get the software

    The host programs and the radio's firmware build from one repository. The firmware is loaded into the radio from your host each time the receiver starts.

    git clone https://github.com/jstockdale/w1700k-sdr
cd w1700k-sdr
make firmware host

  3. Give the host its two addresses

    Replace CONTROL and STREAM with the names of the two interfaces (ip link lists them).

    # the port cabled to lan3
sudo init/host/link keep CONTROL 192.168.1.10/24
# the stream port: its address, and jumbo frames
sudo init/host/link keep STREAM 10.77.0.10/24 9216

    keep writes the settings into the interface's NetworkManager connection, so they survive a reboot and a re-plugged adapter. On the stream port it also raises the receive buffer, and spreads a USB adapter's packets over the CPUs. The jumbo frames matter: each datagram of the stream is 8 KiB, and a port left at an MTU of 1500 drops them all. The software checks this and refuses to start a stream the port would drop.

  4. Give the unit your key

    init/key --known-hosts ~/.ssh/known_hosts

    Run it, then press the reset button with the paperclip and hold. The LED rises white for ten seconds, then flashes and pulses blue. Let go in the blue.

    1. White, risingKeep holding: ten seconds, until it is full.
    2. Blue, pulsingTen seconds: let go now.
    3. Blue, breathingThe unit waits for a key, up to three minutes.
    4. Blue turning greenThe key is in.

    The command sends your public key (~/.ssh/id_ed25519.pub unless you name another), logs in with it to prove it works, and prints the fingerprint of the unit's host key. Check that against your card. With --known-hosts it also records the host key, so ssh root@192.168.1.1 and the tools know the unit from then on.

    A press while the unit waits ends the wait. The key stays across restarts and updates until you change it: see The reset button.

  5. Start the receiver

    host/stream/build/w1700k-stream &
host/spectrum/build/w1700k-spectrum --controller "host/bin/w1700k serve"

    Open http://127.0.0.1:8073/ in a browser on the host and press ▶. The first start loads the firmware and brings the radio up, which takes about two seconds. The LED lifts towards white as the unit hears you, then takes the colour of the stream.

    To watch from another machine, add --listen 0.0.0.0:8073 and open the host's address there. One browser is served at a time.

Reading the LED

The swatches move at the LED's own pace. Green, yellow and red mean go, look and stop.

What you seeWhat it means
Yellow, breathingNo link on the stream port.
White, brighteningPowered and linked. The radio is not loaded, or is loading.
Green, steadyReady: the radio is loaded and tuned, and not streaming.
Cyan, blue or magentaStreaming at 40, 80 or 160 Msps.
The rainbow, round every six secondsStreaming at 320 Msps: everything the receiver has.
… at a steady brightnessLossless: every sample arrives bit for bit.
… breathingThe samples are reduced to fewer bits so the stream fits your link.
… blinking slowlyA stream was started but nothing is leaving the port.
Yellow, blinkingLook: the synthesizer of the band you are capturing is out of lock. Tune back inside the band.
Red, blinkingStop: the stream ended by itself. Your next request clears it.
A short lift towards whiteThe unit heard a change from the host.
Red, never changingThe unit did not finish booting. Cycle its power.

The reset button

The unit keeps one login key, and only the reset button changes it. What a press means is decided when you let go, by how long you held it. The LED shows which stretch you are in, so you never have to count.

While you holdLetting go
First 5 s: green, risingNothing yet. At 5 s it would switch your key on.
First 5 s: red, sinkingNothing yet. At 5 s it would switch your key off.
First 10 s: white, risingNothing yet. The unit has no key, so there is nothing to switch: the white rises all the way to the blue, with no flash at 5 s.
5 s: a flash, then that colour pulsingSwitches the key on or off. Off, it is kept but does not log in.
10 s: a flash, then blue pulsingOpens the slot for a new key (init/key). It replaces the old one.
15 s: a flash, then red blinking hardErases the key.

Rising means on and sinking means off, so the direction tells you even where red and green look alike. Let go before five seconds and nothing happens. If you lose your key, this is the way back in: ten seconds, and init/key with a new one.

What your link carries

The software reads the stream port's speed at every start and packs the samples to fit. It refuses a sample rate that nothing fits and names the speed that rate would need.

Stream linkLossless, all 14 bitsReduced to fit
10 Gbit/s320 Msps
5 Gbit/s160 Msps320 Msps at 14 bits per I/Q pair
2.5 Gbit/s80 Msps160 Msps at 14 bits per I/Q pair
1 Gbit/s80 Msps at 8 bits, 40 Msps at 16 bits

The 10 and 5 Gbit/s rows are measured on units. The 2.5 and 1 Gbit/s rows follow from the same arithmetic and have not been run on such links. Reduced streams are scaled back to 14-bit samples on the host, so your programs see the same format with a little more quantisation noise.

Beyond the browser

The command line

Everything the page does is a command. Tuning is not by channel: the LO goes to any frequency a band's synthesizer locks at, and can move inside a running stream.

host/bin/w1700k load            # bring the radio up
host/bin/w1700k tune 5250M      # or 5.25G, 2441.000123M
host/bin/w1700k gain --agc
host/bin/w1700k start
host/bin/w1700k status
host/bin/w1700k stop

band picks the band and its sample rate, filter the analog filter; host/bin/w1700k --help lists them all.

Your own programs

w1700k-stream serves the samples to every program on the host at once, as complex int16 in shared memory with gaps marked. sdr-recv records them to disk. A SoapySDR module, driver=w1700k, gives the receiver to GNU Radio and other SoapySDR programs; make host builds it where SoapySDR's headers are installed.

Read before use

  • The unit receives only. Its firmware runs none of the transmit calibrations and keeps the transmit path disabled. Even so, do not cable an antenna port straight into a sensitive instrument without an attenuator.
  • The login key belongs to whoever holds the unit. Nobody on the network can change it, and anyone with a paperclip and a cable to the unit can. If a unit comes to you from someone else, give it your own key before you trust it.
  • Keep the card. Its fingerprint is how you know the unit you are talking to is yours.
